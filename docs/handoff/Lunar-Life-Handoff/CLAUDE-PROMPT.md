## Product target
A playable six-minute, top-down junior astronaut mission. Use Among Us as a reference for moving to physical stations and Animal Crossing for approachable dialogue and room warmth. Use original Lunar Life art, characters, layouts and tasks. No imported commercial game art or clone mechanics. The player receives a short briefing before controlling the mission, then completes tasks by walking through a connected habitat. Stay in the world: a station may open a small focused panel that closes back to the same position; no full-screen black dashboard for the mission.

## Playable sequence
1. Preserve astronaut creation. Show a skippable/reopenable short briefing delivered by the commander before the first playable sequence: “Keep the crew breathing. Check the power reserve and the crops. When the radiation alert sounds, reach the shielded shelter.” Show WASD/arrows and E. Keep dialogue short and age-appropriate.
2. Academy: walk to Rivera and press E. In training, show a scripted shadow window and ask the player to keep life support powered by postponing a lower-priority load. Reuse the existing Power Console. Give immediate feedback, a quick retry and the existing certification on success. Walk to exit hatch and graduate; support a separate developer fast entry only if the repo already has a debug mechanism.
3. Outpost: use one connected walkable map with power to the west, greenhouse to the east, shelter to the north and a short central corridor. Show a compact objective tracker. Walk to the power station, inspect battery and choose a reserve policy; walk to the greenhouse, inspect crops and set a conservation plan. Require proximity and a deliberate E press. Give each station distinctive art, an interaction prompt and a visible state change. Briefing and dialogue panels capture movement only while they are open; Escape closes station panels and returns focus. No remote task completion via global menus.
4. After the routine checks, an archived solar energetic particle (SEP) event triggers an alert. Show a text/icon alert and a clear route to the shelter. Let the player walk there, enter a shielded zone and confirm sheltering at a hatch or inside station. Sheltering reduces simulated exposure; the power decision is what to keep on while sheltered. Example competing choices: pause discretionary science, run crops in conservation mode, or pause both to preserve battery. Use actual ResourceSim load identifiers, and tune the choices so they have visible differences over the replay window. Crew safety and life support are hard priorities; crop progress and science are trade-offs. Do not give premature crop failure for a brief pause. Allow ample time on the first run. Keep the simulation's timing model explicit and consistent across open panels, menus and retries.
5. Debrief: show result, battery minimum, life-support uptime, crops/science and simulated exposure with one sentence explaining why. Retry the same incident without losing the astronaut profile; reset transient mission and simulation values. Keep source notes accessible from the debrief. Defer the full library and live API if needed.

## Acceptance checklist
- Import and launch without parse errors. Existing astronaut customization, NPC dialogue and training controls still work.
- Start from a fresh run: briefing → training certificate → enter outpost → approach two stations → alert → walk into shelter → debrief. Entire path works offline without hidden developer actions. E does nothing to distant stations. Walls and props collide; all intended doors can be traversed.
- Test two different load strategies and a late shelter arrival. Results differ for explainable reasons. Retry resets alert, tasks and resource state while preserving the profile. Rapid presses cannot trigger the alert or complete tasks twice.
- At base resolution and integer scale, prompt, warning, objective tracker and station values remain legible. No need to distinguish colors alone. A new player can say why they changed a load and why sheltering helped.
